# Articulated artwork and review status

The original sheets, sprites and source identity hashes remain authoritative.
`articulated/v1/` contains **derived front-view cutout prototypes**, not a claim
that every character has a finished turnaround or a production animation suite.

Nabil/Rayan are the first movement benchmark. The other four legacy-ready rigs
can use the compatible cutout template but need their own visual movement review.
Arif, Sami, Mehna and Tithi still have design references only. They remain
reference-only in the library and cannot render. No generic adult art is used.

For each of those four adults an artist must supply matching front and profile
layers: hips/pelvis, torso, neck, head, front/back hair, brows, eye whites,
pupils, closed eyelids, mouth A–H/X, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, lower
legs and feet. Use the supplied face silhouette, hairstyle, outfit, palette,
accessories, proportions, shading and line weight. Preserve clothing details on
both sides of every joint. Do not paint another character into these folders.

Required pivots in a common untrimmed canvas: pelvis; neck base; head turn;
shoulders; elbows; wrists; hips; knees; ankles; and the ground contact line.
Each joint needs a small hidden overlap, without baking the next limb into it.
Provide neutral/open palm/pointing/fist hand silhouettes, seated lower-body
artwork, and explicit left/right-facing profile heads before claiming full
turnarounds, profile walking, pointing fingers or believable seated poses.

For all six existing cutout prototypes, true separated side/back art and these
alternate hand silhouettes remain missing. A front head tilt is **not** a true
profile view. The software must not fabricate one by squashing a face.

Deliver original layered SVG/PSD/Krita artwork plus transparent PNG exports,
semantic rig version, pivot JSON and SHA-256 hashes. Keep source reference files.
Validate a neutral reconstruction, walk/contact sheet, stop, turn, talking,
one-hand gesture, blink, surprise and listener reaction in a real movie before
changing a character's articulated quality status. Machine frame differences
alone cannot establish natural acting or television quality.
